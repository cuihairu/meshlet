# Personal Networking: Meshlet, ZeroTier, and Tailscale

## Takeaway

Meshlet trades convenience and mature connectivity features for self-hosting and direct control. ZeroTier and Tailscale provide established coordination, client support, and connectivity behavior that a personal project would need substantial work to reproduce.

## Comparison areas

The study compares 61 capabilities across connectivity and performance, networks and groups, service exposure, identity and permissions, platforms, operations, and integrations. It treats perceived speed and connection success as separate measurements: a faster route is not the same as a more reliable connection.

## Practical scope

A feature being technically possible does not make it a sensible first release. The recommended sequence favors core Overlay reliability, small-scale administration, and evidence-backed LAN routing. Peer-to-peer traversal, mobile clients, richer service publishing, and broad identity features need explicit demand and prototypes before they enter the plan.

## How to read the matrix

The source study records each capability, product behavior, personal usefulness, implementation cost, and dependencies. Ratings are estimates, not benchmark results. Any claim about speed or reachability should be tested on comparable paths and devices.
